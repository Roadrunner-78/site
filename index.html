The five

Four you pay for. The fifth is where the paying stops.

  • 1. You stop pastingYour files are too big to paste, so you paste a summary and hope. We stop that. We start with a real job off your desk — one you'd otherwise pay someone for — run against your actual files. I set it up in front of you: your own GitHub account, your name, your card. Then you break one of your files on purpose and put it back, my hands nowhere near the keyboard. If your work lives in Word, I tell you up front what works and what doesn't.
  • 2. You stop repeating yourselfYou explain your business again every session, and it still gets the same things wrong. So: when it gets something wrong, you don't argue with it. You write a rule in your own words, save it, and rerun the job to check whether it fixes the mistake. You write those lines so you can explain and change them yourself. You'll also decide what to keep out. Some work is a one-off. For that, just ask in a chat window like you always did.
  • 3. You do one aloneAt some point you do a whole job in here with me not in the room. Not a practice run — something a client or a partner is waiting for, that you'd have done anyway. You send it to them. They take it and use it. That's the test. I don't mark it. Then you do a different kind of job, because one job working is a demo and two is the real thing. Working well with me next to you proves less than it feels like.
  • 4. You keep it trueYour answers start getting worse, and you assume the AI got worse. First, we check what changed in your files and rules. A note points at a folder you renamed. Two of your rules argue with each other. Something that stopped being true is still sitting in there. You work out which one it is and you fix it. One question does most of the work: is this still true now? If not, delete it — anything committed to your repository can be recovered from its history. If anyone else works with you, they get in here too.
  • 5. You don't need meThere is no separate fee for ending, and no next monthly invoice. You keep the account, the files, the history, the whole thing — none of it was ever mine, and I'm removed from it the day you say so. If you want me after that, you pay me by the hour, by agreement, no standing call. I'll raise this before you do. I'd rather say it out loud than bill you for another month of watching you not need me.

There's no calendar here. You cross each one on your own work, and you'll know you crossed it because you watched yourself do it — not because I said so. You'll be at the last one on one part of your work while you're still at the first on something new. It's finished when you get through the whole list on something I've never seen, and I'll be the one who says it.
